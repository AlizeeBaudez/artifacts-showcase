The story

It started with a borrowed laptop
and one client who needed a website.
€200. A registration number. A business.

In early 2016, I left a comfortable agency role to figure out what working for myself actually felt like. I came back from a month travelling with a direction. Within weeks, I had invoiced my first client — a small retailer who needed a content overhaul — created a company to do it, and landed a retainer managing the editorial strategy for a regional media group. Not quite a grand plan. More like a series of good decisions made quickly.

For two years I held both — a part-time consulting arrangement and my own growing client list — learning on the job, taking on content strategy missions, brand messaging, multilingual copywriting, and increasingly, organic growth consulting. The hot topic at the time was content clusters and topical authority. It feels like a lifetime ago. By the time I went fully independent, the business was already generating 20% of my income on a handful of evenings a week. The maths made sense. I went full-time.

"Autonomy wasn't a perk. It was the non-negotiable I built everything else around."

What followed was two years of working from home offices and co-working spaces across Europe — Lisbon, Warsaw, Tallinn, Vienna, the Azores — building a consulting practice from wherever I happened to be. Doing international content strategy while actually living it. Investing in the craft: attending conferences twice a year, earning certifications, reading obsessively, learning from peers who were further ahead.

2016
First invoice. First registration. First retainer. Three things in the same month, none of them planned together. The business was live.
2016–2018
Two years consulting part-time, building in parallel. Growing a content strategy and organic growth specialisation from real client work — then taking it fully independent.
2018–2020
Full-time. Fully mobile. Home offices across Lisbon, Warsaw, Tallinn, Vienna, the Azores. The business grew every year. The investment in the craft paid off.
2020–2024
Lyon. A home. A pandemic. An industry that transformed more than once. Steady growth through it all. Content clusters gave way to E-E-A-T thinking, then AI-driven search, then a complete rethink of what organic visibility means. Still here. Still curious.
2025
Took a deliberate pause. Had a child. The business waited. So did the clients worth keeping.
2026
Year ten. Back fully. Formalising a decade of pattern recognition into a framework. The work is sharper than it has ever been.

Over ten years, the work has taken me across industries as varied as fintech, higher education, SaaS, retail, food and beverage, hospitality, professional services, e-commerce, and publishing — across markets including France, the UK, Germany, the US, Canada, the Netherlands, and more. The through-line was always the same: helping businesses communicate clearly and build visibility in markets where they aren't yet known.

Markets worked across — 2016 to 2026

None of this happened alone. My partner has shared an office with me for seven of those ten years. Friends celebrated the wins and steadied the harder moments. Clients became collaborators, then people I'm genuinely glad I met. That web of people is part of what made this possible — and part of what makes the work good.

This anniversary isn't a lookback. It's a marker for where I'm going next.

What ten years means for the work

Independence isn't just a lifestyle choice.
It's a structural advantage.

01
No hierarchy between you and the thinking
No account manager filtering the strategy before it reaches you. What you get is the actual analysis, directly.
02
No incentive to recommend what's billable
Independent consultants are paid to give the right answer, not the answer that extends the engagement.
03
Ten years of pattern recognition
From content clusters to AI-driven search. Across markets, languages, and business models. That depth shapes every recommendation.
04
Full accountability to the outcome
One consultant. Fully responsible for the work. No junior team running your account while the senior sells the next one.

The businesses I work with are navigating real complexity — multi-market expansion, fragmented content architectures, brand visibility that needs to travel across languages and cultures. That's not a job for a generic audit. It's a job for someone who has spent a decade thinking about exactly this.
