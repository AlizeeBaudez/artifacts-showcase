→ Executive Summary

What the data is telling you at a glance.

Over the December–February 2027 period, your LinkedIn presence is clearly gaining momentum, but the data reveals a critical tension: posting frequency and post quality are pulling in opposite directions. Your strongest posts — the introduction post (1,044 impressions, 30 reactions) and the content consumption behaviour data post (2,704 impressions) — came when you were posting less often and with more deliberate intent. As February brought near-daily posting, average impressions per post dropped to roughly 100–150, a significant regression from the 350–500 range of December.

The most striking insight is that data-backed posts and genuine personal milestones massively outperform opinion posts and promotional content — often by a factor of 3–4x in both impressions and reactions. The "about me" signature block has minimal impact on engagement rate (2.04% with vs 1.66% without), but the posts with the highest raw impressions consistently don't have it. On format: you're running an all-text strategy, which works well for your audience, with one outlier showing that adding an external link card to a strong piece of external research dramatically amplifies reach.

Biggest win: the content data post (Feb 9)2,704 impressions — 6× your average. Data + external credibility = outsized reach for your audience.

10 Recommendations — What to Do Next

Prioritised by impact. Green = do immediately, Blue = implement over next 4 weeks, Amber = test and iterate.
  • Cut posting to 3× per week max HIGH PRIORITY The data is unambiguous: your per-post impressions dropped ~65% when you went daily in February. Quality > quantity, and LinkedIn's algorithm appears to spread your reach thinner as frequency increases. Pick Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday as your core posting days (best average performance). Let each post breathe for at least 36–48 hours.
  • Find and share external data 2× per month HIGH PRIORITY The content consumption research post got 2,704 impressions (9× your average) by anchoring your POV to third-party data. You don't need to post link cards every time — you can cite stats in text. But identifying 2 compelling data points per month from research papers, industry studies, or Twitter/X threads and building posts around them is your fastest path to organic reach expansion. Your angle on the data is what makes it yours.
  • Write one genuine personal/milestone post per month HIGH PRIORITY Your Dec 21 intro post (30 reactions) and Jan 4 11-year post (highest ER at 3.77%) both outperformed everything except the viral data post. People want to know the human behind the expertise. These don't need to be navel-gazing — they work best when personal experience connects to a strategic insight (e.g., "11 years in → here's what changed about how I work").
  • Restructure the "about me" footer: shorter and more selective MEDIUM PRIORITY Stop using the 3-line version ("Follow for practical takes on content strategy…"). It reads as filler. The 2-line "Reach out if this sounds like what you need" is better — it's direct, not performative. But the most important move: only use the footer on posts where a conversion is the point (client stories, service mentions, Sprint announcements). Remove it from thought leadership, data commentary, and opinion posts. Those stand better on their own.
  • Build more structured framework posts MEDIUM PRIORITY The Jan 3 prioritisation framework post (CRITICAL / IMPORTANT / SECONDARY) got 553 impressions and 17 engagements — 3rd best by interactions excluding the Jan 10 and Jan 26 posts. Structured, numbered, or tiered content is highly shareable on LinkedIn because it's immediately usable. Aim for one per month: a framework, a decision matrix, or a "before you do X, check these Y things" structure.
  • Post at 14:30–15:30 CET (Tue/Wed/Thu) MEDIUM PRIORITY Afternoon posts average 333 impressions vs ~235 for morning posts (once the viral outlier is removed). Your audience is likely in France + UK, working 9-6 CET. Early-mid afternoon catches them mid-day, when they're more likely to pause and scroll. The shift to 9am in February coincided with lower performance — that may be more about frequency, but it's worth testing.
  • Reframe multilingual SEO posts for a broader hook LOW PRIORITY Multilingual SEO posts underperform (avg 256 impressions, 2.9 engagements) despite being a core service. The issue may be audience alignment — most of your engaged followers are content practitioners, not the decision-makers at companies planning international expansion. Try reframing: instead of "here's a technical consideration for multilingual sites," try "here's a business decision most companies get wrong before they go global." Lead with the business consequence, not the SEO mechanic.
  • Avoid pure promotional posts — embed the offer differently LOW PRIORITY The Jan 23 Strategy Sprint promo post (118 impressions — lowest in the dataset) confirms what you probably already know: explicit "I have a spot open" posts underperform. Instead, let client stories, frameworks, and POV posts do the selling indirectly. If you want a direct CTA, bury it: end a strong-performing post type with "details in comments" and put the pitch there.
