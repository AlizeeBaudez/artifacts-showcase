Finding

The quality paradox

Total sleep was actually longest during the health event (6.89h avg) — especially Feb 2026 at 7.5h. But the quality collapsed. More hours, less restoration. The body was maxing out on light sleep while deep and REM dried up.

Finding

Transition month: the lowest ebb

March 2026 — the transition month — shows total sleep 5.88h, deep sleep 0.42h (half the baseline), REM 0.94h (also half). And yet HRV jumped to 49ms. The relief of the acute phase ending registered immediately in the nervous system.

Action

Your sleep recovery path

At 54 weeks into recovery, sleep is still compressed by lifestyle demands. Target: protect your first sleep cycle (11pm–2am) — this is when most deep sleep happens. Consistent wake time is more powerful than consistent bedtime.

Action

REM is where emotional processing and memory consolidation happen. REM is recovering (now ~1.6h) but still below the 1.7h baseline. Late stress and disrupted schedules are the biggest suppressors at this stage. Morning light within 30min of waking is the most accessible lever.

Where you go from here

Your body is
ready for the next chapter.

At 54 weeks into recovery, back at full capacity, managing a business and a busy life — you're not starting from zero. You're starting from experience. Here's what the data suggests you prioritize.

Sleep

Recover your deep sleep

  • Consistent wake time (non-negotiable) — your circadian anchor
  • Morning light within 20–30 min of waking — free and powerful
  • Cut alcohol completely if you haven't — the biggest deep sleep suppressor
  • Keep room cool (18–19°C) — deep sleep is temperature-sensitive
  • No screens 30 min before bed — or blue light filter at minimum
  • As you phase out any remaining disruptions, do it gradually — sudden hormonal shift can worsen sleep before it improves
Activity & Fitness

Rebuild strength, not cardio

  • 2x/week strength training — 20 min minimum, even bodyweight at home
  • Steps are already solid (14–15k/day); steps aren't the problem
  • Prioritize glutes & deep core before heavier lifts — check with a physio if coming off a period of reduced training
  • Lower your Move ring target to 400 kcal — close it every day instead of chasing 95%
  • October showed you CAN hit 18k steps — recreate those conditions
  • Resistance bands or kettlebell: 1 tool, 100 options, fits in short morning windows
Focus & Cognitive energy

Your brain needs targeted recovery

  • REM deficit = reduced working memory — this explains the fog, not failure
  • Protect your first 90-min deep work block each morning as non-negotiable
  • Recovery periods can deplete key nutrients — consider omega-3 and a quality multivitamin
  • Magnesium glycinate 200mg before bed — supports both sleep quality and cognitive function
  • Caffeine cutoff before noon — adenosine builds faster during recovery; afternoon coffee is stealing tomorrow's clarity
  • Short nap (10–20 min) on days with poor night sleep — better than pushing through
