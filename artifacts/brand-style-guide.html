Brand Personality & Identity

Brand Feel

Premium · Grounded · Sharp · Warm · Elevated

Identity Words

European · Strategic · Detail-oriented

Primary Audience

Founders and marketing leads at mid-to-large companies operating internationally

Core Positioning

Strategic peer — not a contractor. The brand leads with clarity, not urgency.

Voice Characteristics

The brand is

  • Direct and precise — no filler words
  • Warm without being casual
  • Confident without being loud
  • European in sensibility — restrained, considered
  • Strategically minded — outcomes over tactics
  • Honest about complexity, never over-simplifying
  • Specific — names markets, problems, results

The brand is not

  • Hyperbolic ("game-changing," "revolutionary")
  • Over-familiar or aggressively casual
  • Keyword-stuffed or SEO-baity in tone
  • Playful or whimsical in professional contexts
  • Vague ("I help businesses grow online")
  • Urgent or pressure-driven in CTAs
  • Agency-like — this is a personal brand

5. Key Squarespace Limitations

Colour themes

Default colour themes for system pages (404, checkout) can only be changed in the Squarespace mobile app, not the desktop editor.

Hover states

Native Squarespace does not support button hover colour customisation without CSS. If hover states matter, one small CSS override is required.

Tertiary buttons

Squarespace has no native tertiary button style. Achieve the underlined text-link style using a text link with manual formatting, or a button styled via the Custom CSS editor.

Font upload

Custom fonts (.woff2, .woff) can now be uploaded natively under Site Styles → Fonts → Upload Font — no CSS injection needed as of late 2025.

Visual Philosophy
Imagery on camille-renard.com is editorial, not decorative. It earns its place by reinforcing a feeling — grounded, considered, European — or it doesn't appear at all. Typography and white space carry the brand; photography supports it. When in doubt, remove the image.
↔

Hierarchy: type first, image second

This is a typography-led brand. Images are used sparingly: one hero image max per page, newsletter cover headers, and social media where visual context adds signal. Internal pages, blog posts, and product pages are predominantly text.

☀

Light: soft, natural, directional

Natural window light. Morning or golden-hour warmth. Soft shadows, never harsh. No studio flash. No artificial-looking overexposed whites. Warm tones that pull from the Teal/Cream palette — not cool or clinical.

◻

Composition: restrained, room to breathe

Negative space is intentional. Never cluttered. Subjects placed with intention — not centred by default. European still-life sensibility: a few carefully chosen objects, arranged with precision. No symmetry for symmetry's sake.

▤

Palette alignment

Images should feel at home in the brand palette: warm creams, off-whites, muted greens, natural wood, terracotta, stone. Images with saturated blues, purples, or neon tones are off-brand. No cold-toned photography.

∅

People: only if it's Camille

No generic stock people. No AI-generated people. If human presence is needed, it must be the consultant herself — in a professional, candid, unposed setting. Hands over faces are acceptable as an abstraction.

✦

AI imagery: high bar, specific purpose

AI-generated images are permitted only when they are photorealistic and indistinguishable from photography, serve a specific decorative or atmospheric purpose, and align with the palette and composition principles above. Never use AI imagery for hero sections where credibility is highest. Preferred for: newsletter banners, social headers, background texture elements.

Preferred Subject Matter

Workspace objects

Open notebooks, fountain pens, ceramic mugs, stacked books with muted covers. Suggests thinking, craft, and deliberate work.

European interiors

Stone, linen, aged wood, arched windows. Signals the geographic sensibility of the brand without being location-specific.

Natural textures

Linen fabric, terracotta, travertine, paper grain. Used as background texture or detail close-up — not as focal scenes.

Botanicals

Single stems in small vases, eucalyptus, dried flowers. Warm-toned — ranunculus, roses, dried wheat — not tropical or brightly coloured.

Abstract light

Window light casting shadows, sunlight through linen, soft bokeh backgrounds. Mood-setting, never the subject itself.

Maps and geography

Vintage maps, atlases, globes — used sparingly as a nod to international work. Antique, not digital.

Imagery Verdicts — What Works vs What Doesn't
✓ Use
Open notebook on a walnut desk, lily of the valley in a glass vase, espresso in a white cup, closed MacBook in background — soft morning light through a linen curtain.
✕ Avoid
Abundance of pink ranunculus, stone millstones, navy blue vases — too lifestyle-coded and palette-inconsistent.
✓ Use
Close-up of fountain pen on open journal page, dappled leaf shadow across paper, terracotta bowl of pebbles to the side.
✕ Avoid
All-teal-toned rustic still lifes with figs and brass vases — beautiful in isolation but reads as food/lifestyle brand, not strategic consultant.
✓ Use
Travertine or linen as a background texture with minimal objects placed with precision — architectural, not domestic.
✕ Avoid
AI-rendered scenes with telltale perfect lighting, travertine disc prop, or colour schemes that feel designed rather than found.

Social Media Visual System

LinkedIn — Thought leadership focus

75% text-led posts (no image). 25% with supporting visual — either a typographic pull-quote card or a restrained still-life image. Carousels: Cream or White backgrounds, Libre Baskerville for main statements, Inter for body. Deep Navy or Teal as accent on slide number or CTA slide.

Instagram — If/when active

Grid should feel cohesive. Alternate between typographic cards (Cream bg + Charcoal type) and still-life photography. Avoid mixing warm and cool-toned images in adjacent posts. No Reels with heavy editing or trend-chasing formats.

Carousel structure (LinkedIn)

Slide 1: Hook — H2-scale statement in Libre Baskerville. Slides 2–N: supporting points in Inter, one idea per slide. Last slide: CTA + camille-renard.com. Max 8 slides. No progress bars or decorative corner flourishes.

Pull-quote cards

Background: Warm Cream (#F6F0EA). Quote in Libre Baskerville Italic, large. Attribution in Inter 400, small, #6B6560. Optional thin Deep Navy rule above attribution. No quotation mark graphics — the italic type signals quotation.

Texture close-up — Background / decorative
Section background · Social card bg · PDF cover texture
Extreme close-up macro photograph of [linen fabric / textured paper grain / stone surface / aged wood grain]. Warm, neutral light. Colours: cream, warm white, faint teal, muted grey. No sharp shadows. Completely abstract — no identifiable objects. Subtle texture that reads as a background, not a subject. Photorealistic. 1:1 or 2:1 aspect ratio.
These work well as very-low-opacity overlays or as full-bleed section backgrounds under type. Ensure text contrast is maintained — test with Charcoal type on top before using.
Seasonal variation — Autumn/winter workspace
Blog cover · Newsletter · Social post image
Photorealistic editorial photograph. A worn oak desk near a rain-streaked window, autumn garden blurred behind the glass. Objects arranged with restraint: a stack of 2–3 books with muted cloth covers (no legible titles), a ceramic speckled mug with black coffee, a closed Moleskine journal, a single dried hydrangea stem in a terracotta vessel. Warm teal and charcoal tones. No pink. No abundance — minimalist arrangement. Natural window light, overcast sky, diffused. Shot in the style of a French or Scandinavian editorial. 1.91:1 ratio.
Map / Geography nod
Organic Growth Strategy content · About page · Blog covers
Photorealistic close-up of an antique or aged paper map, partially unfolded on a pale wooden desk. Warm light from the side. Colours: sepia, off-white, faded navy, aged paper. A mechanical pencil or brass compass rests on the map, casually placed not posed. No digital maps. No GPS imagery. No globes with bright plastic colours. Shallow depth of field, edges soft. European editorial aesthetic. 1.91:1 ratio.
